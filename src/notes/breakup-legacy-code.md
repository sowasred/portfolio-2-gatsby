I spent a good chunk of a relationship treating it like a legacy codebase. Every time something broke, I patched it. Small fix here, workaround there, "we'll clean it up later."

Anyone who's maintained old code knows how that goes. The patches pile up, nobody remembers why things work the way they do, and eventually you're scared to touch anything.

The breakup was basically admitting the rewrite wasn't going to happen. No one did anything terrible. We just kept shipping hotfixes instead of talking about the architecture.

What I'm taking from it: have the hard conversation early, same as flagging tech debt in sprint planning. It's cheaper than the outage.

Also, Ontario winters are long. Go for the walk anyway.
